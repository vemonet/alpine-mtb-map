# Spots that still need trail traces

Working list of the spots in `alpine-mtb-map.kml` that have a pin but no usable geometry: **every spot with 0 or 1 line**. Generated 2026-08-20 from the KML (650 spots: 569 with no line at all, 81 with a single line). Pal Arinsal, SilverStar and Silver Mountain have since been traced and removed, leaving 647; Empire 47 was added as a new spot on 2026-08-21, so 648. Sentiers du Moulin was traced out on 2026-08-21, so 647.

## How to use this file

| Column | Meaning |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Spot** | the `<name>` of the spot placemark in `alpine-mtb-map.kml`, verbatim - grep it to find the pin |
| **Traces** | lines currently attached to that spot (a line is named `<spot base name>: <trail>`) |
| **OSM search** | empty = not searched yet · ✅ = searched (`scripts/trails.py`, name search, GPS trace archive) and nothing usable found |
| **Web search** | empty = not searched yet · ✅ = operator site / official map checked, no GPX, KML or Google My Maps `mid` |
| **Comments** | what was tried, what to try next, and the regional convention that usually decides it |

Rules for keeping it honest:

- Mark ✅ only for a search that was actually run and came back empty. A spot where geometry _was_ found gets its row deleted, not a tick.
- **When a spot ends up with most of its runs drawn, delete its row.** Judge by the network, not by a number: a park with 8 signed trails and 7 lines is done, a 40-trail park with 6 lines is not. Leave the row and update the comment when only a fraction is covered.
- A row with both columns ✅ is not dead - it means the two cheap sources are exhausted and it needs a new one (see the fallbacks in `SKILL.md`).
- Order inside each section is a rough priority: lift-served size, signed trail count and how well known the place is. It is a hint, not a ranking - take whatever you can actually find a source for.
- A "1 trace" spot is not automatically under-mapped. For several Swiss hills the single line is all OpenStreetMap holds; those rows say so.

**An empty OSM column is not evidence that OSM holds nothing.** Pal Arinsal sat here with one line while OpenStreetMap held 24 named runs - mapped as short `name` + `bicycle=designated` fragments that `trails.py` lists individually and that therefore look too small to be trails. At any bike-park row, run `scripts/parkruns.py <bbox>` before believing the OSM column; it groups fragments by name, chains them at both ends and drop-tests the result. That is how the 22 Pal Arinsal lines were added on 2026-08-20.

**At a North American resort, the operator's own PDFs are the classifier.** SilverStar also sat here at one line, and its 60 park runs are mapped as bare `highway=path` + `name` - no `bicycle`, no `mtb:scale`, nothing a tag filter can key on. What separates them from the XC and hiking networks sharing the same hillside is the resort's map set: `cms.skisilverstar.com` publishes the DH bike, cross-country and hiking maps as PDFs, and the cross-country map carries a complete trail-length table. A name in that table is XC, not a park run - that is what removed Brian's, Eric's, Cabin Trail, Silver Shack, Corkscrew, Chakra, Crack of Dawn, Grizzly Adams, Snake Pit and Paradise from the shortlist. 16 lines added on 2026-08-20. Look for the resort CDN's PDFs before trusting names.

**At a North American resort the runs may be mapped as ski pistes, and the operator's own trail list is what tells you.** Sugarbush's bike park is signed down its ski trails, and OSM has them as `piste:type=downhill` - so a query that filters ski pistes out (as `nascan`-style sweeps do, to keep the winter runs out of a bike map) throws away the park. What decides it is the summer map's numbered trail list: Lower Jester, Valley House Traverse, Reverse Traverse, Gondolier, Lower Snowball, Coffee Run and Heaven's Gate Traverse are numbered bike trails **and** ski pistes, while Jester, Castlerock Runout, Racer's Edge and Out to Lunch are ski-only. Fernie is the same story for Summer Road and Cedar Trail. Say so in the description when you draw one - the line is the ski corridor the operator signs as a bike run, not a separately mapped trail.

**Prefer a resort's live trail report over its map PDF.** Fernie's bike map PDF 404s to WebFetch and its summer page carries no name list, but `skifernie.com/conditions/trail-report/` renders all 39 bike trails as text, grouped Elk side / Timber side / base-area XC. That grouping is a better classifier than a PDF legend, because it separates the park from the XC network by the operator's own reckoning. Sugarbush's summer PDF, by contrast, is text-extractable and carries the full numbered list - inflate the streams and read the `(...)` strings, no tooling needed.

**Body text belongs on the blue pin, not on a grey waypoint - and a grey named "valley station" may be sitting on the summit.** Thirteen spots had their whole description (Trails / price / season / access) on a grey waypoint while the blue pin held a one-line caption: Rochers-de-Naye, La Forclaz, Thyon/Veysonnaz, Vatra Dornei, Spicak, Brandnertal, Kaprun, Antur Stiniog, Mount Sima, Nitehawk, Chapelco, St-Luc and Visperterminen. All swapped back on 2026-08-22, and the swap exposed a second fault in eleven of them: the grey's `<name>` said "valley station" / "base area" / "visitor centre" while its own bold header and its coordinate were the **top** of the lift. Those names are corrected. Two more were the placeholder pattern the duplicate-coordinate note describes - `La Forclaz` and `Bikepark Brandnertal` greys sat on the exact coordinate of their own blue pin with a header naming a different, higher place; both got their real lift-top coordinates (Le Tsaté t-bar top 2,202 m; Loischkopf 1,783 m). Rochers-de-Naye had no top-station waypoint at all and its text claimed a 2,042 m top; the OSM station node reads 1,967 m and the summit 2,005 m, so a Rochers de Naye waypoint was added and the vertical corrected from "1500 m" to about 1,565 m. **Check which end of the spot the text is on whenever you touch one** - and read the grey's name against its own altitude, not just against its header.

**A trace does not need a spot id.** Both `main.ts` and `scripts/export.ts` fall back to the placemark's own name (`p.spot || p.name`), so a line with no `spot` Data element still draws, still filters on its own tags and season, and still appears in the trace list - it just has no spot card and no sidebar row. That is the right shape for a trace that belongs to no spot: the two Taney lines carried `spot=taney` with no pin defining it, which read as a broken reference. The id was removed on 2026-08-22 rather than a pin invented.

**A "nothing in OSM" tick is only worth what was actually queried - and `mtb:scale` networks hide from the bike-park selector.** Meteoro Bike Park was written up on 2026-08-22 as having nothing in OpenStreetMap. It has **55 named trails**, every one carrying `mtb:scale`, `mtb:scale:imba` and `mtb:type=allmountain`, imported as ways `1549648xxx`-`1550944xxx`. Two things caused the miss: the sweep that was run was `verify_mtb.py` against a pin **4.9 km off** the real trailhead, and `parkruns.py` selects on `bicycle=designated`/`destination` while this network is `bicycle=permissive`, so it returns zero here. `trails.py` finds it instantly. Patch `parkruns.collect` to select `way["mtb:scale"]["name"]` and it groups, chains and drop-tests normally - that is how the 11 lines were emitted on 2026-08-22. **Run `trails.py` before writing "nothing in OSM", and re-run it after any pin move.**

**Meteoro is also the clearest "closed on terrain" case on the map after Valmont.** The whole hill spans 1,837-1,917 m: 80 m of relief across 55 trails. Only 11 clear the drop test and the biggest is 56 m over 1.3 km at 4%. The grades came from `mtb:scale:imba` (no `colour` tag anywhere), mapped 1/2/3 onto the green/blue/red the park signs - which departs from the Coldwater reading of 3 as black, and does so because the maintainer confirmed the park signs three colours topping out at red.

Blanket sweeps already done, so do not redo them wholesale:

- **2026-08-09** - every then-traceless spot was swept for OSM geometry (`trails.py`-style mtb tag query, nearest-pin assignment, drop test). Rows here that are not ticked either postdate that sweep or need a _different_ query than the mtb-tag one (name-only chains, `bicycle=designated`, `marked_trail`).
- **2026-08-08** Swiss/border thin-trace sweep, **2026-08-19** tier-2 lift-served sweep, **2026-08-20** tier-3 Central/Eastern sweep. Their negative results are in the comments below.

Three duplicate pins turned up while counting: Peyragudes (two pins, same coordinates), Mystic Bright (two pins 2 km apart, one carrying all 22 lines) and Manzherok (two pins 2.3 km apart). **All three are merged as of 2026-08-22** - Peyragudes on 2026-08-21, Mystic and Manzherok on 2026-08-22.

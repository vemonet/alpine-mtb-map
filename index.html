    About this map

    Why?

    Finding a good mountain-bike spot means trawling a dozen resort sites, none of which tell you what you actually need to know in one place: what the trails are like, how the lift works, and the costs. And once you have found somewhere, there is rarely a KML or GPX to drop into your GPS app.

    Websites like Trailforks are useful resources, but it can be difficult to find the riding that matters: the filters do not always narrow the map enough, and the sheer number of routes can obscure the good ones. Many are dirt roads or hiking trails that have somehow been tagged for MTB despite being impractical to ride. In high alpine areas, some mapped routes even approach alpinism rather than mountain biking.

    This map takes a spot-first approach. A typical MTB outing starts by choosing a destination, ideally with a lift and a selection of worthwhile trails to ride throughout the day. Each destination is first represented by a single point, with a practical description of the riding available there. Once you arrive, the trails are often easy to find from signs, local maps or the other riders around you.

    When reliable trail traces are available, they are included as a useful bonus rather than the foundation of the map. Every spot lives in a single KML file you can download and load into CoMaps, Organic Maps or OsmAnd; this site is a convenient way to browse and filter the same data without installing anything.

    The website also provides live weather forecasts and marks spots expected to be wet, using rain on the selected day and the preceding days to help you judge current and upcoming trail conditions. How much rain it takes to flag a spot as wet depends on how its terrain drains: spots tagged rain-sensitive (clay, dark forest soil, little sun) turn wet with a small amount of rain or rain the day before; untagged spots use a slightly less sensitive default; spots tagged rain-resilient (rocky, well-draining, sunny/exposed terrain) only flag as wet after genuinely heavy rain on the day itself; no note means it is assumed to behave like most spots.

    Spots and lines marked Tested and approved have been ridden in person by a map contributor and confirmed as worthwhile MTB riding.

    Where the data comes from
    • Trail geometry from OpenStreetMap MTB route relations, pulled via Overpass and simplified. Indicative only: follow the signs on the ground, not the trace.
    • Lift prices, opening dates, daily hours and travel times compiled from the operators' own sites and Magic Pass, in July 2026. Where published daily hours could not be confirmed, the pin shows a typical regional estimate and says so explicitly.
    • Trail descriptions written by humans often assisted by an LLM, from riding and from operator trail maps.
    • Sources are linked per feature: each spot description and each trail trace includes links to the sources used for its description or geometry, so you can check the underlying information yourself.
    • Base map tiles from OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap (CC BY-SA) and CyclOSM.
    • Weather forecasts from Open-Meteo. A blue rain badge means at least 1 mm of precipitation or a 50% probability is forecast, or at least 5 mm fell the previous day; these are the thresholds for rain-sensitive spots. Untagged spots need a bit more rain, and rain-resilient spots only badge on heavy same-day rain, as explained above.

    Natural trail grading: T and E

    Ratings such as T5 / E2 combine two independent VTTour scales. T describes how technically difficult the riding is; E describes how serious the consequences of a fall are. T5 / E2 therefore means extremely difficult riding on a narrow trail with some obstacles, but without the severe cliff exposure of E3 or E4. Ratings assume dry conditions and remain subjective.

    Technical difficulty
    T1 Wide, smooth trail; gentle slope below roughly 20%; broad turns and no obstacles.
    T2 Wide trail; moderate 20-30% slope; fine loose stones, small obstacles and easy switchbacks.
    T3 Narrow and often steeper than 30%; roots, steps below 30 cm, medium scree and a few difficult switchbacks.
    T4 Narrow, steep and strongly off-camber; steps above 30 cm, large loose rock and repeated difficult or occasional trials-style turns.
    T5 Extreme gradients with numerous trials-style passages requiring advanced balance, wheel placement and low-speed control.
    Exposure and fall consequences
    E1 Low exposure: wide trail with no major hazards beside or below it.
    E2 Medium exposure: narrow trail with some obstacles that can worsen a fall.
    E3 High exposure: very narrow trail with many obstacles, steep slopes or small rock drops beside it.
    E4 Very high exposure: substantial cliffs or rock drops beside the trail; a fall can have critical consequences.

    Scale definitions adapted from VTTour's grading reference.

    Contributing

    Spots are missing, and prices go stale. Add yours to public/alpine-mtb-map.kml, check it renders with vp i && vp dev, and send a pull request. The README has a template and the tag reference.
